eCVT actuator testbench

A hardware-in-the-loop rig that finds shifter failures on the bench instead of on race day.

When
Aug 2025 to May 2026
Where
UCLA Bruin Racing Baja SAE
Role
Electronics project lead

A standalone rig to test the car's electronic CVT actuator under controlled, repeatable loads before it ever goes into the vehicle.

Contributed to a 51.4% improvement in our Baja competition event performance.

How it works

  • A primary motor drives the shifter while a second motor applies a controllable load. Both run on ODrive controllers over CAN and I2C.
  • Feedback comes from an encoder, optical limit switches and a gear tooth sensor, logged alongside ODrive telemetry on a Raspberry Pi.
  • Bring-up went in steps: signal validation, then baseline tests, then stress tests over many cycles.
  • Pass criteria: repeatable shifts, stable under load, no stalling.

What broke, and the fix

Missed gear-tooth pulses
The sensor gap was wrong. Re-setting it brought the signal back.
Fine at no load, failing under load
The motor didn't have the torque for realistic shifting loads, and the bench proved we needed more.
Trusting the controller's own numbers
Added an external encoder so we could compare commanded input against actual output.

Why it mattered

It moved validation earlier, so failures turned up before race day. It gave the electronics and powertrain teams one shared platform for PID tuning and debugging.
