Underactuated Pendubot

Two links, one motor. Balancing the free link through the dynamics of the driven one.

When
2026
Status
In progress

The shoulder is driven and the elbow swings free, so the controller has to balance the passive link entirely through the dynamics of the active one. I'm building it to compare four control strategies on the same hardware.

The design

  • Two equal 6061-T6 aluminum links, each 11 in long, 2 in wide and 1/4 in thick, with masses and inertias taken from CAD.
  • Direct drive with link 1 bolted to the motor shaft and no gearbox, which removes backlash at the joint that matters most.
  • Absolute encoders on both joints. Encoder 2 rides on link 1 through a flexible coupling so it doesn't side-load its bearings.
  • A custom motor-control PCB running a real-time control loop in embedded C++.
  • Nonlinear equations of motion and a state-space model, simulated in MATLAB/Simulink before any hardware runs.

Test plan

  1. Stabilization. Start hanging down and compare settling time, overshoot, steady-state error and oscillation.
  2. Disturbance rejection. Apply a repeatable impulse and measure peak deflection, recovery time and motor torque.
  3. Robustness. Add mass to a link and see which controller copes with the model being wrong.
  4. Compute cost. Execution time, jitter and processor load: PID and LQR against NMPC.

Designing for failure

Hanging down is the stable rest state, so cutting power is safe. The danger is the fall: link 2 has no rotation limit and can swing hard, and a runaway controller can wind up link 1's encoder cable. The plan: a software limit on link 1, cable strain relief and a cutoff when balance is lost.
