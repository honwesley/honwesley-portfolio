Nonlinear MPC for a DC microgrid

Holding a shared DC bus steady through constant-power load steps with four boost converters.

Constant power loads are destabilizing: when the bus voltage dips, the load draws more current to hold its power, which pulls the voltage down further. I designed a nonlinear model predictive controller that sets the duty cycles of several parallel boost converters to hold a shared DC bus at its reference through large load steps.

The bus recovers to its reference in under 0.1 s after load steps, and often under 20 ms in the switching-model simulation.

Building the model up in stages

  1. One averaged converter with a CPL. Verified the steady state: duty cycle 0.669, zero state derivatives.
  2. Open loop. Fixed duty cycle, then a 5 kW to 8 kW load step to watch the bus sag.
  3. Simple duty controller. Used to confirm the plant behaves before adding anything clever.
  4. Nonlinear MPC. Cost, state and output functions written by hand.
  5. Four converters, one bus. States are every inductor current plus bus voltage, inputs are every duty cycle, and the cost adds current sharing.
  6. Switching models. Averaged converters replaced with MOSFET, diode and PWM models and compared against the averaged version.

Constraints the controller respects

  • Duty cycle between 0.05 and 0.90.
  • Inductor current within its maximum.
  • Bus voltage between its minimum and maximum.

The board

Four boost stages, each with a current sensor feeding the controller, which runs the optimization and drives the MOSFET PWM. An external supply comes in through a buck and an LDO for the logic rails, with low-pass filtering on the power lines. The boost stages sit on one edge, the supply in the bottom left and the controller in the bottom right. I did the bring-up with scopes, differential probes and electronic loads, chasing gate ringing, conduction losses and VDS switching transients.
